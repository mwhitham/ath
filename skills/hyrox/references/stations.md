# The eight stations

Read this file when the athlete asks how to do, train, or replace a station, or when
a review needs the standard behind a slow or penalised station. Stations are in race
order.

Labels used here:

- **Rule (book p.)**: an official 2026/27 rule, with its rulebook and page.
- **Practice**: common coaching practice. No HYROX study tests it.
- **Substitution**: a replacement when equipment is missing. It always changes the
  stimulus, so say what changes and do not compare its times with race times.

## Loads by format (2026/27)

Each station below gives the Singles loads in the order Women / Men / Women Pro /
Men Pro (Singles 26/27 p. 8). Doubles and Relay use the same load sets:

| Load set | Sled push / pull | Farmers carry | Sandbag | Wall ball | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Women (white) | 102 / 78 kg | 2 × 16 kg | 10 kg | 4 kg | Singles Women; Doubles Women; Relay Women |
| Men and Women Pro (grey) | 152 / 103 kg | 2 × 24 kg | 20 kg | 6 kg | Singles Men and Women Pro; Doubles Men, Women Pro, and Mixed; Relay Men |
| Men Pro (black) | 202 / 153 kg | 2 × 32 kg | 30 kg | 9 kg | Singles Men Pro; Doubles Men Pro |

The colour is the colour of the race equipment (Singles 26/27 pp. 32–35). Sled loads
include the sled. Wall-ball targets are 2.70 m for women and 3.00 m for
men in every format (Singles 26/27 p. 35; Doubles p. 37; Relay p. 24). SkiErg, row,
burpee broad jumps, and the 100 wall-ball reps are the same in all three formats.
Relay Mixed loads are not stated (Relay 26/27 p. 8). Adaptive divisions differ; see
[adaptive.md](adaptive.md). Full tables are in
[rules-2026-27.md](rules-2026-27.md#divisions-and-loads).

## How to progress any station (Practice)

Move one stage at a time. Stay at a stage until the athlete meets its check twice in
different weeks.

| Stage | What it means | Check before moving on |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Technique | Light load or short distance, fresh, filmed when possible | No faults a judge would call; the athlete can say the standard |
| 2. Fresh capacity | Race load or distance, fresh, in intervals | Race work done in sets with steady times |
| 3. Under fatigue | The station after a run or another station | Time within about 10–15% of the fresh time, with no extra faults |
| 4. Race pace | Inside race combinations or a simulation | Planned time held, and the next run on plan |

The percentage in stage 3 is a coaching judgment, not a tested threshold. Use the
athlete's own numbers once they exist.

## 1. SkiErg, 1,000 m

**Rules (Singles 26/27 p. 26; Doubles p. 27; Relay p. 15; Adaptive p. 24)**

- A judge resets the monitor. Both feet stay on the platform. Heels may hang over
  the edge but must not touch the floor. Jumping is allowed if the feet land on the
  base plate.
- After 1,000 m, stay on the platform and raise an arm until the judge confirms.
- Damper "preset to resistance 6 for all divisions"; the racer may change it as
  often as they like (Singles 26/27 p. 26). The Relay book has no damper note.

**Faults and penalties:** a foot touching the floor: warning, then 15 s per fault.
Leaving early: DQ (Singles 26/27 p. 45).

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Start tall with arms high. Pull down by hinging at the hips and bending the knees
  a little. Finish with the hands past the hips.
- Let body weight and the hinge drive the pull. Arms alone tire fast.
- Return tall under control. Keep one stroke rate you can hold.
- The first 200 m is easy to overdo with race excitement. Hold back there.

**Progression (Practice):** 4–6 × 250 m with short rest → 3 × 500 m → 1,000 m
steady → 1,000 m straight after a 1 km run.

**Substitutions:** a rower or bike erg for aerobic work; band or cable straight-arm
pull-downs with a hip hinge for the pattern. Both change the stimulus: different
muscles, no SkiErg flywheel, and no monitor distance to pace against.

## 2. Sled Push, 4 × 12.5 m

**Rules (Singles 26/27 p. 27; Doubles p. 28; Relay pp. 15–16)**

- Use the assigned lane. Sled and racer start fully behind the white line.
- Stay in your lane. The whole sled must pass the 12.5 m mark "entirely before
  changing direction".
- The station ends when four lengths are done and the whole sled is past the line.
- Loads include the sled: 102 / 152 / 152 / 202 kg (Singles 26/27 p. 27).

**Faults and penalties:** sled not fully over the line, or leaving the lane:
warning, then 15 s per fault. A missing length: DQ (Singles 26/27 p. 45).

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Arms straight or slightly bent on the poles. Body low and long.
- Short, quick steps through the balls of the feet.
- Keep the sled moving. Starting a stopped sled again costs more than keeping it
  going.
- At each end, check the whole sled is past the line, then change sides quickly.

**Progression (Practice):** light, fast pushes for position → race load, 4 × 12.5 m
with full rest → race load after a 1 km run → short pushes above race load when the
athlete is strong enough and the surface allows.

**Equipment note:** floor friction differs by venue and gym. In sled towing, surface
friction changed how much added weight slowed athletes (Transferable: Linthorne and
Cooper 2013, 6 rugby players). A sled time in the gym does not predict the race
time. Record the sled's own weight, the plates, and the
surface each time, and compare only like with like.

**Substitutions:** a non-motorised or switched-off treadmill push, a heavy
prowler-style push on a different surface, or heavy incline walking with a weight
vest, plus leg strength work (split squats, leg press). All change the stimulus:
different friction, speed, and body angle.

## 3. Sled Pull, 4 × 12.5 m

**Rules (Singles 26/27 pp. 27–28; Doubles p. 29; Relay pp. 16–17)**

- Start fully inside the Racers Box and stay in it at each end.
- Do not step on the front or back line of the box while holding the rope.
- The whole sled must pass the 12.5 m mark before changing direction. Then walk or
  run to the other end and pull it back.
- Stay standing. No pulling while seated or kneeling.
- The rope must stay in your lane; it may trail out of the back but must not get in
  a neighbour's way.
- Loads include the sled: 78 / 103 / 103 / 153 kg (Singles 26/27 p. 28). Event
  chalk is allowed here (Singles 26/27 p. 45).

**Faults and penalties:** warning, then 15 s per fault. A missing length: DQ.

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Feet wide and planted. Lean back a little.
- Pull hand over hand with long pulls. Use the hips and legs to keep the rope tight
  between pulls.
- Lay the rope behind you in a pile inside your lane. Do not stand on it.

**Progression (Practice):** rope technique with a light sled → race load with full
rest → race load after the sled push and a run.

**Substitutions:** a rope tied to a loaded sled or bag dragged on turf; hand-over-hand
rope pulls on a cable machine; heavy rows. These change the stimulus: the race sled
starts from still each length, and friction differs.

## 4. Burpee Broad Jumps, 80 m

**Rules (Singles 26/27 pp. 29–30; Doubles pp. 30–31; Relay pp. 18–19)**

- Start with a burpee, both hands behind the start line and chest on the ground.
  "'chest on the ground' is defined as the nipple line making clear contact with
  the ground."
- Jump or step out of the burpee, then broad jump. Both feet take off and land
  together; one foot may be at most 5 cm ahead of the other.
- "Additional steps or shuffles are not permitted at any time."
- A knee may help you up. The feet must not land beyond the fingertips when you
  step or jump in.
- Hands go no more than 30 cm in front of the toes of the front foot, measured from
  the base of the palms. Once down, the hands cannot move forward.
- Both feet must fully cross every white start and finish line. If either foot
  lands on the finish line, do one more burpee broad jump.

**Faults and penalties:** warning, then 15 s per fault (code 10). Common faults:
chest not touching, feet not together, extra shuffle steps, hands too far ahead.

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Many athletes step back and step up to save the legs. Choose the version that
  keeps the rhythm steady.
- Jump a distance you can repeat. A moderate jump with a quick next burpee is
  usually faster over 80 m than long jumps with long pauses.
- Land softly with feet together, then go straight down.

**Progression (Practice):** 4 × 10 m → 2 × 40 m → 80 m → 80 m after a 1 km run.
Runs after this station were slow in one small study (see
[evidence.md](evidence.md)), so practise the run that follows.

**Substitutions:** none needed for equipment. With little space, go back and forth
on a short lane. Shorter lanes add turns, which change the rhythm.

## 5. Rowing, 1,000 m

**Rules (Singles 26/27 p. 30; Relay p. 19)**

- A judge resets the monitor. Stay seated until 1,000 m is done and the judge
  confirms.
- You may stop, put the handle in the holder, and rest, but you may not stand up.
- Damper preset to 6 and adjustable. Footplates preset to position 4; adjust them
  "before beginning".

**Faults and penalties:** warning, then 15 s per fault. Leaving the rower early: DQ.

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Legs, then body, then arms on the drive. Arms, body, then legs on the return.
- Set the footplate and straps before the first stroke.
- Use the row to settle breathing before farmers carry, but keep a steady split.

**Progression (Practice):** 4 × 250 m → 2 × 500 m → 1,000 m → 1,000 m after
a 1 km run and burpee broad jumps.

**Substitutions:** SkiErg or bike erg. Different muscles and no rowing split to
pace against.

## 6. Farmers Carry, 200 m

**Rules (Singles 26/27 pp. 31–32; Doubles p. 33; Relay p. 20)**

- Pick the right weight: 2 × 16 / 24 / 24 / 32 kg.
- Take the kettlebells from the marked box and return them to it.
- Carry both kettlebells while moving, arms extended by your sides.
- You may put them down to rest if they do not move forward when placed.
- Return them to the correct box with handles upright.
- The 200 m may be several laps. Learn the athlete map and count your laps.
- Event chalk is allowed here.

**Faults and penalties:** kettlebells returned wrongly or not upright: 30 s, unless
fixed before leaving the Roxzone. Wrong weight: repeat the station or DQ. Missing a
lap: DQ.

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Pick up with a flat back. Stand tall. Look ahead.
- Short, quick steps. Breathe steadily.
- If grip is failing, set the bells down early and briefly rather than dropping them.

**Progression (Practice):** 4 × 50 m → 2 × 100 m → 200 m → 200 m after the row
and a run.

**Substitutions:** dumbbells, a trap bar, or loaded bags. Handle shape changes the
grip demand.

## 7. Sandbag Lunges, 100 m

**Rules (Singles 26/27 pp. 32–33; Doubles pp. 34–35; Relay pp. 21–22)**

- Pick the right sandbag: 10 / 20 / 20 / 30 kg.
- Start standing tall with both feet behind the line.
- In every lunge "the trailing knee must clearly touch the ground". Each rep ends
  standing tall, knees and hips fully extended. Alternate legs.
- You may pause between lunges with both feet parallel. No steps or shuffles.
- The bag "must remain fully supported on both shoulders". It must not rest on the
  ground or anything else.
- No lunging around turning points. Each section restarts with both feet behind
  the white line.
- Return the bag to the marked area.

**Faults and penalties:** knee not touching, or not standing tall: warning, then
15 s each (code 14). Bag off the shoulders: 15 s each (code 15). Bag not returned:
30 s unless fixed before leaving the Roxzone. Wrong weight: repeat or DQ.

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Settle the bag high across the upper back and both shoulders before the first
  step.
- Medium steps. Touch the knee gently each rep. Stand fully tall each rep, because
  a half stand is the most common call.
- Plan short, standing pauses rather than slowing every rep.

**Progression (Practice):** bodyweight walking lunges → 4 × 25 m with the bag → 2 ×
50 m → 100 m → 100 m then a 1 km run. Lunges load the knee, and knee problems are
the most common injury in one HYROX cohort (see [evidence.md](evidence.md)). Raise
lunge volume slowly. If the knee hurts, stop lunging and advise the athlete to see a
clinician or physiotherapist.

**Substitutions:** a loaded backpack worn high, or a light barbell on the upper back.
A rigid bar is more stable than a sandbag and the arms work less.

## 8. Wall Balls, 100 reps

**Rules (Singles 26/27 pp. 34–36; Doubles pp. 36–38; Relay pp. 23–25)**

- Pick the right ball: 4 / 6 / 6 / 9 kg. Targets: 2.70 / 3.00 / 2.70 / 3.00 m.
- Start each set standing tall, holding the ball in both hands. Picking the ball up
  and throwing it straight away is not allowed.
- Squat until the hips are lower than the knees, then throw with both hands so the
  ball strikes the correct target.
- After the ball hits the target, catch it and go again, or let it fall and restart
  from standing tall. You may not catch it after a bounce and keep going.
- Judges and the physical target decide valid reps. Digital screens are only "a
  visual aid" (Singles 26/27 p. 35).
- Shoes may come off at the target and must go under the rig (Singles 26/27 p. 34).
- Powdered chalk at wall balls: 2 min (Singles 26/27 p. 36).

**Faults and penalties:** no warnings. Each fault is a no-rep: squat not deep
enough, ball not hitting the strike zone, starting a set from the floor, or catching
after a bounce. Each no-rep adds one more throw. Leaving early: DQ.

**Technique cues (Practice)**

- Catch the ball high and ride it down into the next squat.
- Drive up through the legs, then let the arms finish the throw.
- Break the 100 into planned sets before failure, with short planned rests. The
  right sets depend on the athlete; test them in training.
- Look at the target, not the floor.

**Progression (Practice):** 5 × 20 fresh → 4 × 25 → 3 × 35 → 100 for time → 100
after lunges and a 1 km run.

**Equipment note:** a gym target is often 9 ft (2.74 m) or 10 ft (3.05 m). These
are close to, but not the same as, 2.70 m and 3.00 m. Measure the gym target and
note it in the planned session.

**Substitutions:** with a ball but no target, mark the correct height on a safe,
solid wall. With no ball, do dumbbell thrusters or squat-to-press. Thrusters remove
the throw and catch, which changes rhythm and shoulder work.

## Missing equipment: summary

| Missing | Substitution | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| SkiErg | Rower, bike erg, band pull-downs | Muscles, pacing feedback |
| Sled (push) | Switched-off treadmill push, other surface | Friction, speed, body angle |
| Sled (pull) | Rope with a dragged load, cable rope pulls | Start from still, friction |
| Rower | SkiErg, bike erg | Muscles, pacing feedback |
| Kettlebells | Dumbbells, trap bar, bags | Grip shape |
| Sandbag | Loaded backpack, light bar | Stability, arm work |
| Wall ball or target | Marked wall, thrusters | Throw, catch, rhythm |

Tell the athlete when a substitution is in the plan. Write it in the planned
session's block instructions. Log a simulation that used substitutions under its own
benchmark definition, as described in [records.md](records.md).
