# Adaptive HYROX

Read this file when the athlete races, or wants to race, in an Adaptive division.

## The rule for this skill

Work only from the Adaptive division the athlete says HYROX confirmed. Never assign a
division, suggest which one fits, or say whether the athlete is eligible. Medical
questions about an impairment go to the athlete's clinician.

The reason: the rulebook says racers "must be officially classified into one of the
above divisions to ensure eligibility. These divisions are reserved for individuals
with permanent impairments." (Adaptive 26/27 p. 15). "Racers with temporary injuries
(e.g., due to knee or hip surgery) do not qualify" (p. 15). The book does not say who
classifies, how, or what evidence is needed (pp. 6–15, 52–56). Only HYROX's process
can place an athlete.

What you may do:

- Ask: "Which Adaptive division did HYROX confirm for you?" Record the answer in the
  result's extension as `adaptive_category`, for example `"Neurological Minor,
  confirmed by HYROX"`.
- If the athlete has not been classified, point them to HYROX. The book says: "we
  encourage you to contact us directly" when an impairment does not fit a listed
  division (p. 15).
- Plan training within any limits set by the athlete's clinician or physiotherapist.
- Tell the athlete that Classification Codes arrive "By the mid-season" and that "a
  mid-season review will take place" (pp. 52, 54). Rules may change during the
  season. Check the current Adaptive rulebook before the race.

What you must not do:

- Infer a division from a diagnosis, a description, or a video.
- Tell an athlete with a temporary injury to enter Adaptive.
- Give medical advice about an impairment, a prosthesis, or a wheelchair.

## Divisions (Adaptive 26/27 pp. 9–15)

Thirteen divisions, differentiated "by gender, repetition and/or weight" (p. 9):
Lower Limb Major, Lower Limb Minor, Upper Limb Major, Upper Limb Minor, Short Stature,
Vision Impairment, D/deaf or Hard of Hearing, Neurological Major, Neurological
Moderate, Neurological Minor, Seated With Hip Function, Seated Without Hip Function,
Seated Without Core Function. Waves start by gender (p. 9).

## What changes by division

Look up the athlete's confirmed division. Values are women / men where two are given.

### Running or pushing distance (p. 21)

Full distance for all divisions except: Short Stature 1 lap; Neurological Minor 2
laps; Neurological Moderate 2 laps; Neurological Major 1 lap. "1 LAP" and "2 LAPS"
have no metre value; they depend on the venue's lap length (p. 21).

### Standards that differ (pp. 24–41)

- Seated racers keep their feet on the footplate. The sled is strapped to the chair
  for the push; racers bring their own straps and declare them (p. 26).
- Lower Limb racers may do the sled pull seated (p. 28).
- Burpee broad jumps: a split stance, extra steps, shuffles, or repositioning the feet
  before the jump are not allowed (pp. 30–31).
- Farmers carry: seated racers carry one kettlebell on the lap with one hand on it;
  single-arm or crutch users carry one kettlebell (p. 34).
- Lunges: seated racers lower the bag to one side, return it to the lap, and push two
  grid lengths, alternating sides; racers whose knee bend is limited may walk with
  crutches carrying the bag (pp. 36–37).
- Wall balls: seated racers start with the ball on the lap; Lower Limb and
  Neurological Major racers get a 60 cm box for stability; Upper Limb and Vision
  Impaired racers may squat without throwing (pp. 39–41). Digital targets count valid
  reps and no-reps in Adaptive, and racers go once the screen shows the reps (p. 40).
- A rowing machine is separated for seated racers (p. 32).

### Penalties that differ (p. 50)

Adaptive uses time penalties where other formats disqualify:

| Fault | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Metres not completed on SkiErg or rower | 15 s per metre in the station text (pp. 24, 32); "MIN PENALTY 10 SECS TO DQ" in the table |
| Missing sled lane | 3 min per lane |
| Missing farmers carry lap | 3 min per lap |
| Missing wall-ball rep | 15 s per rep |
| Chalk outside sled pull and farmers carry | 2 min |
| Not using the assigned equipment or lane at wall balls | 2 min |

The book still disqualifies for missing a whole station or a 1 km run (p. 48).

### Equipment and guides (pp. 19–21)

- Declare all specialised equipment at registration (p. 19). Equipment "must not
  provide a mechanical advantage that exceeds the natural movement of a racer".
- "No time allowances or deductions will be given for the setup, attachment, removal
  or handling of adaptive equipment." (p. 20) Practise equipment changes as part of
  training, because they count in race time.
- Vision Impaired racers use a non-elastic tether of about 50 cm (p. 20).
- Any racer may have a guide runner, handler, or support person for orientation and
  navigation, chosen by the racer. Guides may not help with station work (p. 21).

## Coaching an Adaptive athlete

1. Confirm the division, and the date it was confirmed.
2. Read the distances, loads, and standards for that division from the tables above.
   Plan to those, not to the standard divisions.
3. Ask the athlete which movement versions they will use, and practise those exactly.
4. Work within limits set by their clinician or physiotherapist. Ask them to check
   the plan with that person when a movement or load is new.
5. Rehearse equipment set-up and changes against the clock.
6. Recheck the Adaptive rulebook before the race, because mid-season changes are
   announced.

Log results with `"division": "adaptive"`, `"rulebook": "HYROX Rulebook Adaptive
26/27"`, and `adaptive_category` in the extension. Use one benchmark definition per
confirmed division and category.
