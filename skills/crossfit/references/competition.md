# Competition preparation

Rules change every season. Name the season and the rulebook behind every rule you
state. When the current season's rulebook is not yet published, say so and say
what must be checked. "Rx" means the workout as prescribed. "Scaled" and
"Foundations" are CrossFit's easier official versions.

### Check before you advise

Before giving any competition rule to the athlete, check the current official
rulebook for:

1. the season and rulebook version, with its publication date;
2. the athlete's division, and the date used to set age;
3. release times and submission deadlines for each workout;
4. whether a workout can be repeated and which score counts;
5. the Rx, Scaled, and Foundations versions, and their loads for the athlete's
   age group;
6. time caps, tiebreak points, and how capped scores rank;
7. judging and video rules;
8. adaptive divisions and who administers them.

If you cannot check, say: "This is the 2026 rule. The 2027 rulebook may change it.
Check games.crossfit.com/rules before you rely on it."

## Divisions (2026)

- **Official.** Division is set by birth date and gender. For 2026, age is taken as
  of 14 July 2026. There are 22 individual divisions and one team division
  (2026 Rulebook §1.11).
- **Official.** Individual: Men and Women. Age groups: Boys and Girls 14–15 and
  16–17; Men and Women 35–39, 40–44, 45–49, 50–54, 55–59, 60–64, 65–69, and 70+
  (§1.11).
- **Official.** Teens 16–17 and masters 35–54 do the same Rx workouts as the
  individual division. They rank on the overall Open leaderboard and on their
  age-group leaderboard (§1.30).
- **Official.** The 14–15 and 55+ divisions can have different Rx loads or
  standards. In Open 26.1, Rx men 16–54 threw a 20 lb ball to a 10 ft target; Rx men
  55+ threw the same ball to a 9 ft target (26.1 scorecard).

### Adaptive athletes

- **Official.** "The Adaptive-athlete season will be administered by WheelWOD." The
  Adaptive Open runs at the same time as the Open (§7.01).
- **Official, 2027.** The 2027 Semifinals page lists 16 adaptive divisions, such as
  upper impairment, lower impairment, neuromuscular, visual impairment, short
  stature, seated, and intellectual divisions.
- **Not confirmed.** The full 2026 adaptive division list. WheelWOD's rulebook was
  not read.
- **Rule for this skill.** Never assign an adaptive division or decide eligibility.
  Use only a classification the athlete says the organiser confirmed. Point the
  athlete to the organiser for classification.

## Rx, Scaled, and Foundations (2026)

- **Official.** Each Rx workout "may be accompanied by scaled or foundations
  versions". Athletes may choose a different version for each workout (§1.29).
- **Official.** Scaled athletes rank among Scaled athletes "but below all athletes
  who performed that workout as prescribed". Foundations athletes rank "below all
  athletes who performed that workout as scaled and as prescribed" (§1.29).
- **Official.** A missing score counts as zero and ranks below every athlete who
  entered a score (§§1.26, 1.29).
- **Official.** An athlete who cannot reach an Rx range of motion may not swap the
  movement or use outside help. They "should consider submitting the score in the
  scaled or foundations tier" (§1.19).
- **Official.** "Only CrossFit may provide official scaled versions of workouts."
  Changing the format "will void an athlete's score" (§1.17).
- **Official.** Foundations loads can be suggestions. The 26.3 Foundations scorecard
  says athletes doing every workout in Foundations "are free to decrease or increase
  the load as your skill level allows".

**Practice.** Choosing a version for one workout:

1. Can the athlete meet every Rx movement standard? If not, Scaled (§1.19).
2. If yes, will they complete a meaningful part of the workout before the cap? A
   few reps of the first movement is still a valid Rx score. Check what the athlete
   wants: an Rx score, a placing, or a good experience.
3. Rx always ranks above Scaled in the same workout. A low Rx score can still rank
   higher than a high Scaled score.
4. Decide before the workout. Write down the reason.

## Scoring, caps, and tiebreaks (2026)

- **Official.** Capped athletes rank by reps, with ties broken by tiebreak time.
  Athletes who finish under the cap "will not enter a tiebreak score" (26.1 and 26.3
  scorecards).
- **Official.** A penalty can push a time past the cap. The score then becomes the
  reps done inside the cap. The rulebook's example: Fran with a 6-minute cap,
  submitted at 5:30 with a 60-second penalty, scores 81 of 90 reps (Online
  Semifinals rule, §4.11 and Appendix E). The Open's own penalty rules are covered
  under judging below.
- **Official.** Each workout sets its own tiebreak point, written on its scorecard.
- **Official.** The overall Open rank is the sum of workout placings. Lower is
  better. Placings 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 beat 1 + 2 + 10 = 13. Overall ties go to the best
  single-workout placing, then the next best (§1.25).
- **Official.** "Ties will not be broken for single workout results." Equal results
  share the rank (§1.25).
- **Official.** Advancement uses the worldwide leaderboard. Region views are "simply
  a leaderboard filter" (§§1.27–1.28).
- **Practice.** A finish always ranks above a capped score. Never convert a capped
  score into an estimated time.

## Judging and video (2026)

- **Official.** A score is validated either by a judge at a licensed affiliate in
  good standing, or by a public YouTube video (§1.20).
- **Official.** Affiliate judges need no credential during the Open. Online review
  judges need the current Judges Course or Advanced Judges Course certificate
  (§1.18).
- **Official.** Affiliate scores need no video. They are validated automatically
  unless the affiliate manager rejects them by the stated deadline (§§1.21, 1.26).
- **Official.** Video review outcomes are "Good Video", "Valid With Penalty", "Zero
  (0) Score", and "Invalid". Invalid removes all of the athlete's scores for the
  competition (§1.23).
- **Official.** A score may be changed for bad angles or lighting, fisheye lenses, a
  blocked view, standard violations, clothing that hides the standards, or
  miscounted reps (§1.22).
- **Official.** Video requirements whose breach "may result in a 0 score": show that
  the weights are correct, show setup measurements, follow the rep scheme, submit
  unedited video, and use any camera position the floor plan requires
  (Appendix D).
- **Official.** Video best practice: a three-quarter view, the camera at least 3 ft
  (90 cm) off the ground, a clock counting up from zero visible throughout, and no
  fisheye lens (Appendix D).
- **Official.** Barbells: 45 lb for men, 35 lb for women. Official conversions
  include 95 lb = 43 kg, 135 lb = 61 kg, 225 lb = 102 kg, 53 lb kettlebell = 24 kg,
  and 20 lb ball = 9 kg (Appendix D).
- **Official.** Later online stages use scripted time penalties for no-reps and a
  10-second penalty for a false start. Open video penalties "may vary by movement
  and context" (§§1.23, 4.11).

## Preparing for the Open

### What the evidence covers

- **Research.** Taper research comes from other sports and predates CrossFit
  research. In a meta-analysis of 27 studies, the best taper lasted about two weeks,
  cut volume by 41 to 60%, and kept intensity and frequency (Bosquet et al., 2007).
  The 2027 Open has three workouts, 27.1 to 27.3, over about three weeks. That is
  not one peak day. Treat any taper as **Practice** borrowed from that research.
- **Research.** What predicts Open ranking is not established. The studies are
  small (Butcher et al., 2015; Mangine et al., 2020).
- **Research.** Competitors reported more injuries than non-competitors in
  surveys. This is an association (Montalvo et al., 2017; Rodríguez et al., 2022).

### A season outline

This outline is **Practice**. Fit it to the athlete's assessment.

The taper figure comes from Bosquet et al. (2007): over about two weeks, cut volume
by 41 to 60% and keep intensity and frequency. That research is from other sports
and one peak event. **Hypothesis**, to state when you use it: the Open is three
weekly efforts, so a long cut of that size before and during it could cost
fitness. A coach may choose a smaller cut. Say that it is a departure and why.

| Period (2027 season) | Aim |
|---|---|
| Mid-November to December | Build the athlete's weakest likely Open skills and capacity. One or two priorities. |
| January | Practise Open-style workouts: past Open workouts, movement standards, pacing, and break plans. Register (from 7 January 2027). Practise with a judge or on video. |
| Two weeks before 27.1 | Cut volume by 41 to 60% and keep intensity and frequency (Bosquet et al., 2007), or a smaller cut labelled as a hypothesis. No new skills. |
| Each Open week | Plan the attempt day, a lighter day before it, and recovery after. Keep total volume lower than usual. |
| After the Open | Review every score. Plan the next block from what the Open exposed. |

### Each Open week

1. **Read the scorecard** on release. Note the cap, the tiebreak point, the
   standards, and the loads for the athlete's division and age group.
2. **Choose the version** with the procedure above.
3. **Plan the pacing.** Use the athlete's unbroken set sizes. Plan sets and rests
   for each round. Plan where the tiebreak falls.
4. **Practise the standards.** A short warm-up set under a judge's eye. Check range
   of motion and lockouts.
5. **Set up video** if not judged at an affiliate: angle, height, clock, no fisheye,
   weights shown.
6. **Decide about a repeat.** Whether a repeat is allowed, and which score counts,
   is set by the season's rules. Check them. **Research.** Blood markers and a simple
   test were back to baseline within 48 hours after single CrossFit workouts in 12
   trained people (Timón et al., 2019). This is small and not a rule.
7. **Record the score** with the `org.athleticstandard/crossfit-score` extension:
   `variant`, `competition` (for example "2027 CrossFit Open 27.1"), `judged`,
   `time_cap_s`, `capped`, and `tiebreak_s`. See [records.md](records.md).

### Comparing Open scores

- **Practice.** Compare Rx with Rx, Scaled with Scaled, and Foundations with
  Foundations. The tiers are separate (§1.29).
- **Practice.** Do not compare Foundations scores between athletes unless the loads
  are known, because Foundations loads may be changed.
- **Practice.** Compare age-group scores only against the same Rx loads. 16–17 and
  35–54 match the individual Rx. 14–15 and 55+ may not.
- **Practice.** Affiliate scores are checked less strictly than reviewed video. Treat
  a single score as having some error.
- **Practice.** Report an Open result as a percentile within the division and
  version, not only as a rank. Field sizes change each year.
- **Practice.** Treat an Open workout as a repeat of an earlier one only when
  CrossFit's own announcement says it is. Then check that the version and standards
  match before comparing (see [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md)).
