## 2. Race structure

### Basic format

- "HYROX consists of a 1 km run followed by 1 station, repeated 8 times." (Singles p. 4; Doubles p. 4; Relay p. 4; Adaptive p. 4)
- Racers must do the runs and stations in order: "[Run 1, workout station 1, Run 2, workout station 2, etc.]". They finish after "a total of 8 km running and 8 workout stations". (Singles p. 22; Doubles p. 23; Relay p. 10; Adaptive p. 18)
- A valid finish also needs the racer to start at the designated start time. They must wear the issued timing chip "on an ankle at all times". (Singles p. 22; Doubles p. 23; Relay p. 10; Adaptive p. 18)
- The running distance is the same in every division: 1 km before each station, 8 km in total. (Singles p. 4, p. 8; Doubles p. 4, p. 8; Relay p. 4)
- Adaptive is the exception. Some Adaptive divisions run or push fewer laps. See section 8. (Adaptive p. 9, p. 21)

### Run segments and laps

- The run portion "totals 8,000 meters, divided into eight run segments of approximately 1,000 meters each." (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 20)
- A run segment may be several laps, because of the venue layout. The first or last lap of a segment may be a different length. Any difference "will be offset within that segment's lap structure" so the full 8,000 m is run. (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24–25; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 20)
- Doubles adds: "The running splits are always around 1,000 meters; depending on the venue, this may be split between 1-5 running laps." The first or last lap "may be less than 1,000 meters due to the location of the start tunnel. In these cases, these missing meters will be balanced out during the last lap." (Doubles p. 25)
- "Racers are solely responsible for counting their own running laps; completing extra laps does not warrant any time adjustment, and any lap screen provided at the venue serves only as a non-official aid." (Singles p. 22; Relay p. 11; Adaptive p. 18). The Doubles book does not include this sentence (Doubles p. 23).

### Fast Lane

- A line splits the run course into two zones: "Fast Lane [smaller, on the inside line] and Running Lane [larger, on the outside line]". (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 25; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 20)
- "Racers running at a 4/km pace or faster are required to use the Fast Lane, while other racers should stay in the Running Lane." (Singles p. 23–24; Doubles p. 25; Relay p. 12–13). Adaptive says "running/pushing at a 4/km pace or faster". (Adaptive p. 20)
- If HYROX crew are directing the Fast Lane, all racers must follow their instructions. (Singles p. 24; Doubles p. 25; Relay p. 13). The Adaptive book leaves out this sentence (Adaptive p. 20).
- **Note:** "4/km" is printed with no unit. It most likely means 4 minutes per km, but the book does not say so.

### Roxzone

None of the four books defines the Roxzone. None says where it starts or ends. Everything the books do say is listed here:

- The course combines "the Run Course, Roxzone and workout station distances". (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 20)
- Racers must not enter "the Roxzone or run course before their official start time", because it "may trigger their timing chip and result in an incorrect race time". (Singles p. 22; Doubles p. 23; Relay p. 11; Adaptive p. 18)
- The Roxzone has an IN arch and an OUT arch. Running into the Roxzone through the OUT arch, or out of it through the IN arch, costs 2 minutes each time. (Singles p. 43; Doubles p. 45; Relay p. 32; Adaptive p. 48)
- Each workout station also has an "IN" arch. Leaving a station through its IN arch costs 2 minutes. (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 19)
- A kettlebell or sandbag return error is not penalised if the racer fixes it "before exiting the Roxzone". (Singles p. 32–33; Doubles p. 33, p. 35; Relay p. 21–22; Adaptive p. 35, p. 37)
- Water is available "at least once during, before or after each pass through the Roxzone". (Singles p. 40; Doubles p. 42; Relay p. 29; Adaptive p. 45)
- Littering on "the Roxzone floor or run course" is banned. (Singles p. 40; Doubles p. 42; Relay p. 29; Adaptive p. 45)
- At the World Championships HYROX may change "the design of the race course and Roxzone". (Singles p. 14)

### Timing and splits: what the books actually say

None of the books lists official split types. They do not mention run splits, station splits, or a Roxzone time as results fields. The phrase "timing mat" does not appear. Here is every timing statement:

- Each racer gets "a timing chip for recording the racer's time[s] during the race". It must be on an ankle. "Any other position may lead to an invalid or incomplete time record". (Singles p. 41; Doubles p. 43; Relay p. 30; Adaptive p. 46)
- Not wearing the chip while racing gives a "Did Not Start" [DNS] result. (Singles p. 22, p. 45)
- "Many penalties are automatically detected by the timing system using chip reads—for example, incorrect use of the IN/OUT arches or missing sled laps." (Singles p. 43; Doubles p. 45; Relay p. 32; Adaptive p. 48)
- Doubles teams carry 2 chips. Relay teams carry 4 chips. (Doubles p. 43; Relay p. 30; Adaptive p. 46)
- Doubles finish time: "recorded when both team members have crossed the finish line; the later timing chip read determines the final result". (Doubles p. 10)
- Relay: a member doing two legs back to back must "run through the TZ after each workout to ensure the timing chip is 'seen'". (Relay p. 13)
- Wall-ball digital screens show rep counts. In Singles, Doubles, and Relay they "do not determine the validity of repetitions". (Singles p. 35; Doubles p. 38; Relay p. 24). Adaptive differs. See section 4.8.
- Results are "published immediately after each event ends" at results.hyrox.com. (Singles p. 10; Doubles p. 10; Relay p. 9; Adaptive p. 16)
- "Penalties may be added or amendments made for up to 48 hours after each event ends." (Singles p. 10; Doubles p. 10; Relay p. 9; Adaptive p. 16)
- The only uses of the word "split" are Doubles ("The running splits are always around 1,000 meters", Doubles p. 25) and Relay ("The RELAY split remains the same across all divisions", Relay p. 8). Neither is about timing data.

**Note:** split fields in a results file come from the timing provider, not from the rulebook. A skill should not claim the rulebook defines them.

## 4. Movement standards for the eight stations

These are the standard rules from the Singles book. Doubles, Relay, and Adaptive extras are listed under each station.

General rules for all stations:

- A run or movement that "deviates from the prescribed standards, appears unusual or uncommon, or appears to provide a competitive advantage will not be recognised". It may give a no rep or a time penalty. (Singles p. 25; Doubles p. 26; Relay p. 14; Adaptive p. 23)
- The Head Judge and team assign equipment and lanes. "Racers are not permitted to choose their own equipment or lane under any circumstances." (Singles p. 25; Doubles p. 26; Relay p. 14; Adaptive p. 23)
- On Lunges, Burpee Broad Jumps, and the Farmers Carry, faster racers may overtake when safe. Grid lines there are "orientation only and are not mandatory to follow". (Singles p. 25; Doubles p. 26; Relay p. 14; Adaptive p. 23)
- "Each workout station must be completed in full. Any failure to complete a workout station in its entirety will result in disqualification [Incomplete Station]." This applies to all eight stations. (Singles p. 25; Doubles p. 26; Relay p. 14). The Adaptive book does not have this rule. It uses time penalties for missing work instead. (Adaptive p. 23–41)
- Relay adds that this rule "replaces any minimum-working-distance, minimum-working-requirement, or partial-completion penalty previously associated with an individual station." (Relay p. 14)
- Stations 1–7: one warning per station. On the second infringement the rep is invalid and the time penalty applies. Further faults add more penalties. "Certain violations may result in immediate penalties without warning". (Singles p. 44; Doubles p. 46; Relay p. 33; Adaptive p. 49)

### 4.1 SkiErg (1,000 m)

Rules (Singles p. 26; Doubles p. 27; Relay p. 15; Adaptive p. 24):
- A Judge resets the monitor before the start.
- Both feet must stay on the SkiErg platform. Heels may hang over the edge but must not touch the floor.
- Jumping or dynamic motion is allowed. Feet must land back on the base plate.
- If either foot touches the floor, that is an infringement.
- After the distance, the racer stays on the platform and raises an arm. They may leave only after the Judge confirms.
- The damper is "preset to resistance 6 for all divisions–this may be adjusted as many times as desired by the racer." (Singles p. 26; Doubles p. 27; Adaptive p. 24). The Relay SkiErg section has no damper note (Relay p. 15).

Penalties:
- First foot fault: formal warning. Second: 15 seconds. Each later fault: 15 seconds more. (Singles p. 26; Doubles p. 27; Relay p. 15; Adaptive p. 24)
- Leaving the SkiErg early: "DQ — FAILURE TO COMPLETE 1,000 METERS [INCOMPLETE STATION]". (Singles p. 45; Doubles p. 47; Relay p. 34)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 27):
- Only the working partner may adjust the damper: "by the working DTM [only]". DTM means Doubles Team Member.
- The resting partner stays in the marked area.
- "It is not allowed to pass the SkiErg handles from one DTM to the other."

Adaptive extras (Adaptive p. 24–25):
- "Seated racers are required to have their feet on their footplate at all times".
- At the end, "both feet/wheelchair" stay on the platform.
- "For every meter not completed a 15-second penalty will be applied."

### 4.2 Sled Push (50 m, 4 x 12.5 m)

Rules (Singles p. 27; Relay p. 15–16; Adaptive p. 26):
- Use the sled lane a judge assigns.
- Sled and racer start fully behind the white line.
- Sled and racer stay in their lane and do not interfere with the next lane.
- The sled must pass the 12.5 m mark "entirely before changing direction".
- The station ends when 4 x 12.5 m lanes are done and the whole sled passes the line.

Penalties:
- First fault: warning. Second: 15 seconds. Each later fault: 15 seconds more. (Singles p. 27; Doubles p. 28; Relay p. 16; Adaptive p. 26)
- Missing a sled lane: "DQ — MISSING LANE [INCOMPLETE STATION]". (Singles p. 45; Doubles p. 47; Relay p. 34)
- In Adaptive, a missing lane is 3 minutes per lane instead. (Adaptive p. 26, p. 50)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 28):
- "The resting DTM must walk immediately behind the working DTM."
- If the walking partner obstructs others, for example by walking in a neighbouring lane, it is an infringement.
- The Doubles list leaves out the "stay within their designated lane" rule. It does keep "Each pair of racers will be assigned a sled lane".

Adaptive seated extras (Adaptive p. 26):
- The sled is strapped to the chair. The racer drags the front of the sled over the white line.
- Racers bring their own straps and tell staff at registration and before the start.

### 4.3 Sled Pull (50 m, 4 x 12.5 m)

Rules (Singles p. 27–28; Doubles p. 29; Relay p. 16–17; Adaptive p. 28):
- Use the assigned lane.
- Sled and racer start fully inside their "Racers Box".
- The racer must stay in the Racers Box at each end of the lane.
- "Racers are not permitted to step on the solid line at the front ... or back of the Racers Box ... while holding the rope."
- The sled must pass the 12.5 m mark, "completely over the white line", before changing direction.
- After pulling the whole sled past the mark, the racer runs to the other end and pulls it back.
- The racer must stay standing. No pulling while seated or kneeling.
- The rope must stay in the racer's lane. It may extend out of the back, but must not impede a neighbouring lane.
- The station ends when 4 x 12.5 m lanes are done and the whole sled passes the line.

Penalties:
- Same warning-then-15-seconds protocol. (Singles p. 28; Doubles p. 30; Relay p. 17; Adaptive p. 29)
- Missing lane: DQ (Singles p. 45). Adaptive: 3 minutes per missing lane. (Adaptive p. 28–29, p. 50)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 29):
- "The resting DTM must remain behind the working DTM at all times and may NOT assist with rope management in any way e.g. with their hands or feet."
- "It is permitted for the resting DTM to offer verbal and physical guidance to the working DTM so long as they do not step onto any of the Racer's Box lines, or within the Racer Box itself."

Adaptive extras (Adaptive p. 28):
- The no-seated rule applies "unless explicitly allowed by the movement standards for the respective division."
- "Racers in the Lower Limb division can adapt as follows: Perform the Sled Pull Seated."
- Seated divisions: the front of the sled must pass the 12.5 m mark. The racer stays in the Racers Box. Feet stay on the footplate.

### 4.4 Burpee Broad Jump (80 m)

Rules (Singles p. 29–30; Doubles p. 30–31; Relay p. 18–19; Adaptive p. 30–31):
- Start with a burpee.
- The first burpee of each section: both hands on the ground behind the white start line, chest on the ground.
- "'chest on the ground' is defined as the nipple line making clear contact with the ground."
- The racer may jump or step out of the burpee, then broad jump forward.
- In every broad jump, both feet take off and land at the same time. The front of one foot may be at most 5 cm ahead of the other, at take-off and landing.
- "Additional steps or shuffles are not permitted at any time."
- A knee may be used to come out of the bottom of the burpee.
- When jumping or stepping out of a burpee, the feet must not go beyond the fingertips ("overstepping").
- Once the hands are on the ground they cannot be moved forward.
- For later burpees, hands go "no more than 30 cm forward of the toes of their front foot" (Singles p. 29). The 30 cm is measured from the base of the palms where the hands meet the wrists.
- In the bottom of every burpee, the chest must clearly touch the ground.
- Grid lanes are a guide only. Racers may cross lanes to overtake.
- Jump length is the racer's choice.
- At turning points, the racer need not do the movement around them. Both feet must fully cross every white start/finish line. Each new section starts chest-down with hands behind the white line.
- The station ends when the racer jumps over the finish line. If either foot lands on the finish line, one more burpee broad jump is needed.

Penalties:
- Warning, then 15 seconds per fault. (Singles p. 30, p. 45 code 10; Doubles p. 31, p. 47; Relay p. 19, p. 34; Adaptive p. 31, p. 50)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 31):
- "The first DTM must start by completing a burpee."
- The resting partner walks behind the working partner. Walking alongside is an infringement.
- At a change-over, "the resting DTM will begin by placing their hands where the working DTM's feet landed, toes and fingers in line." Not doing so is an infringement.
- The station ends when the working partner jumps over the finish line.

Adaptive extras (Adaptive p. 30–31):
- "A split stance, where one foot is entirely in front of the other so that the feet no longer overlap, is not permitted."
- "Additional steps, shuffles, or repositioning of the feet prior to the jump are also not permitted."
- Adaptive versions of the movement are in the table in section 8.

### 4.5 Rowing (1,000 m)

Rules (Singles p. 30; Relay p. 19; Adaptive p. 32):
- The judging team resets the monitor before the start.
- The athlete must stay seated until the full 1,000 m is done.
- Racers may stop, put the handle in the holder, and rest with feet on or off the foot plates.
- The athlete may not stand up, leave the seat, or dismount until 1,000 m is done and a judge confirms.
- The damper is preset to Resistance 6 for all divisions and may be changed "as many times as desired by the racer".
- Footplates are preset to "Position Number 4". Racers may adjust them "before beginning".

Penalties:
- Warning, then 15 seconds per fault. (Singles p. 31; Doubles p. 32; Relay p. 20; Adaptive p. 32)
- Leaving the rower early: "DQ — FAILURE TO COMPLETE 1,000 METERS [INCOMPLETE STATION]". (Singles p. 45; Doubles p. 47; Relay p. 34)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 32):
- The Doubles list leaves out "must remain seated on the rower until the full 1,000 meters are completed". It keeps the rule that "The athlete may not stand up, leave the seat, or dismount the rower until the 1,000 meters are completed". See the ambiguity list.
- The resting partner stays in the marked area behind the working partner and must not obstruct others.
- Partners must not pass each other the handle, adjust the damper, or adjust the footstraps for each other.
- Only the working partner may adjust the damper.

Adaptive extras (Adaptive p. 32):
- For seated divisions, "The Rower will be separated so that the Racer can use the machine while seated". Feet stay on the footplate. Racers may ask for extra weights to stop the chair moving.
- "For every meter not completed a 15-second penalty will be applied."

### 4.6 Kettlebell Farmers Carry (200 m)

Rules (Singles p. 31; Doubles p. 33; Relay p. 20; Adaptive p. 34):
- The racer must choose the right kettlebell weight for their division.
- The station starts and ends with taking the kettlebells from, and returning them to, the marked box by the start/finish line.
- The racer carries both kettlebells at all times while moving.
- Arms are extended by the racer's sides.
- The racer may put the kettlebells down to rest, if they do not move forward when placed.
- The station ends when the racer carries the kettlebells over the finish line and returns them to the correct box "ensuring that the handles remain in the upright position".

Penalties:
- Kettlebells not returned to the correct area, or not upright: 30 seconds. No penalty if fixed before leaving the Roxzone. (Singles p. 32; Doubles p. 33; Relay p. 21; Adaptive p. 35)
- Wrong weight for all or part of the carry: the racer must repeat the whole station with the right weight, or be disqualified. (Singles p. 32; Doubles p. 34; Relay p. 21; Adaptive p. 35)
- Missing a Farmers Carry lap: "DQ — MISSING LAP [INCOMPLETE STATION]". (Singles p. 45; Doubles p. 47; Relay p. 34)
- Adaptive: "Each missing lap will result in a 3-minute penalty." (Adaptive p. 35, p. 50)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 33):
- At a change-over, kettlebells "must not be passed forward. Sideways or backwards is permitted, never forwards."
- The resting partner walks behind the working partner. Walking alongside is an infringement.

Adaptive extras (Adaptive p. 34):
- Seated: one kettlebell on the lap. One hand stays on it. The other hand pushes the wheelchair. HYROX provides a lap mat.
- Single-arm profile or crutch users: carry one kettlebell in one hand, crutch in the other. They may choose to carry two under the standard rules.

### 4.7 Sandbag Lunge (100 m)

Rules (Singles p. 32–33; Doubles p. 34–35; Relay p. 21–22; Adaptive p. 36):
- The racer must choose the right sandbag weight.
- The station starts and ends with taking the sandbag from, and returning it to, the marked area by the start/finish line.
- Start standing tall with both feet behind the line, then lunge across it.
- In each lunge "the trailing knee must clearly touch the ground".
- Each rep ends standing tall, knees and hips fully extended.
- Lunges alternate knees.
- The racer may lunge without stopping, or stop between lunges "with both feet parallel on the ground".
- No steps or shuffles between reps.
- The front foot must completely cross the white line at the end of each lane and the finish line.
- No lunging around turning points. Each working section restarts with two feet behind the white line, then a lunge across it.
- The station ends when the leading foot completely crosses the finish line. The sandbag then goes back to the marked area.

Penalties (Singles p. 33; Doubles p. 35; Relay p. 22; Adaptive p. 37):
- First fault: warning. Second: 15 seconds.
- Sandbag not returned to the correct area: 30 seconds. No penalty if fixed before leaving the Roxzone.
- Wrong weight: repeat the whole station or be disqualified.
- "the sandbag must remain fully supported on both shoulders by the working athlete." It must not rest on the ground, barricades, feet, or any other surface. Each fault: 15 seconds.
- Penalty table: "TRAILING KNEE NOT TOUCHING THE GROUND/ NOT STANDING TALL AT LUNGES" = 15 seconds per fault; "REMOVAL OF SANDBAG FROM SHOULDERS" = 15 seconds per fault. (Singles p. 45)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 35):
- At a change-over, the sandbag "must not be passed forward. Sideways or backwards is permitted, never forwards."
- The resting partner walks behind. Walking alongside is an infringement.

Adaptive extras (Adaptive p. 36–37):
- Seated: start still behind the white line with the sandbag on the lap. Lower it to one side until it touches the ground. Return it to the lap. Do 2 grid-length wheelchair pushes, each starting and stopping at a white line. Repeat on the other side. Alternate sides throughout. "The total distance is 50 m for all seated divisions."
- Racers whose profile limits or prevents knee bending, "particularly those using crutches", may carry the sandbag on their shoulders while walking with crutches.

### 4.8 Wall Balls (100 reps)

Rules (Singles p. 34–36; Doubles p. 36–38; Relay p. 23–25):
- The racer must choose the right ball weight.
- Start standing tall, hips and knees extended, holding the ball with both hands. Picking the ball up from the ground and throwing it straight away is not allowed.
- Do a full squat and throw with both hands so the ball strikes the correct target. That is one rep.
- "female racers must hit their target in the strike zone, male racers must hit their target in the strike zone."
- After the ball hits the target, the racer catches it and goes again, or lets it hit the ground and restarts from standing tall. "Racers are not permitted to catch the ball after it bounces and continue with the next repetition."
- In the bottom of the squat, "hips must descend lower than their knees [also known as; below parallel or below 90°]".
- Without digital targets, the racer may go to the Finisher Stage only after the Judge confirms 100 valid reps.
- Racers may take off their shoes at the target and put them under the rig. They must take the shoes to the Finisher Stage. They may not return to the wall-ball area after finishing.
- Racers should avoid running under the rig.
- Breaking any of these rules makes that rep a "no rep".
- Digital screens are "a visual aid" and "do not determine the validity of repetitions". After 100 valid reps the screen turns green and shows "GO". "The physical target and judge confirmation remain the official standard for all repetitions." (Singles p. 35; Doubles p. 38; Relay p. 24)
- A judge may use a squat depth box. Racers cannot request one. It is not for resting or sitting. (Singles p. 36; Doubles p. 38; Relay p. 24; Adaptive p. 41)

Penalties (Singles p. 36; Doubles p. 38; Relay p. 25; Adaptive p. 41):
- "No warnings are given, it is either a rep or a no-rep."
- Powdered chalk at the wall-ball station: 2 minutes.
- Leaving wall balls early: "DQ — FAILURE TO COMPLETE 100 WALL BALLS [INCOMPLETE STATION]". (Singles p. 45; Doubles p. 47; Relay p. 34)
- Adaptive: "Each missing rep will incur a 15-second penalty." (Adaptive p. 41, p. 50)

Doubles extras (Doubles p. 36):
- The resting partner stays in the marked area under the rig without obstructing others. If not, reps are no-reps "for as long as the infringement occurs".
- Change-over: let the ball go to the floor, or hand it over. "A flying transition where one DTM throws the ball to the target and the other DTM catches it in the squat and keeps moving is not permitted." It gives a no-rep.

Relay extras: see section 7 (team joins the last member at the wall-ball station). (Relay p. 25)

Adaptive extras (Adaptive p. 39–41):
- Seated: the ball starts on the lap and must touch the lap between reps. Feet stay on the footplate.
- Lower limb divisions and Neurological Major: a 60 cm plyo box is provided "to assist with stability while performing the squat movement".
- Upper Limb and Vision Impaired: may hold the ball and squat without throwing. They may choose to throw under the standard rules.
- Digital targets work differently in Adaptive. "the system will automatically register both valid reps and no-reps". Judges assess the start position and squat depth and record no-reps on their interface. Racers go to the Finisher Stage "once they confirm their digital interface shows the required repetitions". (Adaptive p. 40)

### Penalties stated in the text

- Missing run laps: "a time penalty of 3-7 minutes per lap will be applied and added to their final result time." Example: 5 minutes per lap at one venue; 2 missed laps turn 1:24 into 1:34. (Singles p. 43; Doubles p. 45; Relay p. 32; Adaptive p. 48)
- Stations out of order: racers may complete missing stations "prior to entering station 8 [Wall Balls]". One out-of-order station costs 3 minutes. More than one means automatic disqualification. (Singles p. 43; Doubles p. 45; Relay p. 32; Adaptive p. 48)
- "Missing an entire station, failing to complete any workout station in full, or missing a 1K run will lead to a disqualification." (Singles p. 43; Doubles p. 45; Relay p. 32). Adaptive says only "Missing an entire station or 1K run will lead to a disqualification." (Adaptive p. 48)
- Leaving a workout station through its "IN" arch: 2 minutes. (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 19)
- Using equipment, a lane, or a wall-ball station not assigned by the Race Crew: 2 minutes per infringement. (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 19)
- Chalk: only event-supplied chalk, only at the stations where it is provided. Removing chalk or carrying it elsewhere: 2 minutes each time. (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 19)
- Aid-station fluids are "for drinking only". Pouring them over the head or body: 2 minutes each time. (Singles p. 23; Doubles p. 24; Relay p. 12; Adaptive p. 19)
- Littering "can result in a 2-minute time penalty". Spitting or clearing nostrils on the floor: "Each infringement will result in a 2-minute time penalty." (Singles p. 40; Doubles p. 42; Relay p. 29; Adaptive p. 45)
- Staying in the start tunnel after the wave starts "may result in a penalty". (Singles p. 24; Relay p. 13; Adaptive p. 22). Penalty table: 1 minute "CAN APPLY". (Singles p. 45)
- Accepting drink or food from anyone except aid stations is "outside assistance". "time penalties can be applied per infringement". (Singles p. 40; Doubles p. 42; Relay p. 29; Adaptive p. 45)
- Handing permitted items to or from anyone "constitutes outside assistance and may result in disqualification". (Singles p. 38; Doubles p. 40; Relay p. 27; Adaptive p. 43)
- Poor sportsmanship "can lead to penalties, disqualification, a lifetime ban from HYROX races and/or legal action". Penalties may be applied after the race. (Singles p. 39, p. 46; Doubles p. 41, p. 48; Relay p. 28, p. 35; Adaptive p. 44, p. 51)
- Doubles, partner running ahead: 1 minute. More than 3 "not staying together" penalties: Out Of Competition, no ranking. (Doubles p. 25)
- Relay, back-to-back member not running through the Transition Zone: "an automatic penalty". The size is not given. (Relay p. 13)
- Adaptive, undeclared equipment that gives "a significant performance jump": the Head Judge or Race Director "may issue a time penalty or in severe cases a Disqualification (DQ)". (Adaptive p. 19). The appendix says instead "the Head Judge may adjust their rank". (Adaptive p. 56)

### Who decides

- Judges or Head Judges can penalise movement and rep faults in real time. (Singles p. 44)
- "Only Race Directors have the authority to disqualify [DQ] a racer". (Singles p. 44; Doubles p. 46; Relay p. 33; Adaptive p. 49)
- "All decisions of the Lead Race Director are final." (Singles p. 24; Doubles p. 25; Relay p. 13; Adaptive p. 22)

### Disqualification (DQ): the full list from the books

- Starting in an earlier or later wave without approval. (Singles p. 22, p. 45)
- Missing a run or a station. (Singles p. 43, p. 45)
- More than one station done out of order. (Singles p. 43, p. 45)
- Any incomplete station: leaving SkiErg, rower, or wall balls early; missing a sled lane; missing a Farmers Carry lap. Not in Adaptive. (Singles p. 25, p. 45)
- Missing a lap when the run segment is a single-lap setup. (Singles p. 45)
- Wrong weight at Farmers Carry or Lunges and not repeating the station. (Singles p. 32–33, p. 45)
- Possible DQ, at the Race Director's choice: spitting, unsporting behaviour, handing items, poor sportsmanship. (Singles p. 38–39, p. 45)
- Adaptive: leaving SkiErg or rower early may be DQ "depending on the severity". Undeclared equipment may be DQ "in severe cases". (Adaptive p. 19, p. 50)

Effect of DQ: no results data, no rankings or awards for that race, and "Once disqualified, the participant may not continue competing in that specific race." (Singles p. 44; Doubles p. 46; Relay p. 33; Adaptive p. 49)
No refund for a DQ or ban. (Singles p. 46)

### Other result statuses

- DNF: "If a participant does not finish a station they will not receive any results data and will be excluded from all rankings and awards. However, they may continue with the race [without a final time]." (Singles p. 44; Doubles p. 46; Relay p. 33; Adaptive p. 49)
- DNF on medical grounds: a Race Director may withdraw a racer if their "blood, vomit, urine, or faeces poses a welfare or contamination risk". This is recorded as DNF. (Singles p. 44; Doubles p. 46; Relay p. 33; Adaptive p. 49)
- DNS: not wearing the timing chip while racing. (Singles p. 22, p. 45)
- Out of Competition: any adaptation of movements, weights, or reps for pregnancy or injury. The racer still gets a finish time but is excluded from rankings and qualification. (Singles p. 5–6; Doubles p. 5–6; Relay p. 5–6). The Adaptive book has the pregnancy rule only. (Adaptive p. 5)
- Out of Ranking: HYROX may declare an event, day, or part of a day "Out of Ranking" for World Championship and/or Elite qualification if venue or weather conditions materially affect performance or safety. (Singles p. 10; Doubles p. 10; Relay p. 9; Adaptive p. 16)

### Time limits and cut-offs

- None of the four books sets an overall race time limit, a station time cap, or a mid-race cut-off. (Searched all four books.)
- The only "cut-off" is for Elite qualification: the leaderboard cut-off date "falls approximately 21 days prior to day one of each elite event". (Singles p. 15–16, p. 21; Doubles p. 15, p. 21)

## 6. Doubles rules

### Start and team

- "A HYROX Doubles Team must consist of two racers. Both racers must be present at the start line and must begin the race together. Starting the race with only one racer is not permitted" (Doubles p. 22)
- Each team gets an official start time. Racers must show it on their arm. (Doubles p. 23)
- Each partner wears their own chip: "Doubles = 2 Timing Chips". (Doubles p. 43)
- Registered partners "cannot be changed or substituted" for World Championship slots. (Doubles p. 11–12)

### Running together

- "Both Doubles Team Members [DTMs] must run together for the entire 1,000m between each workout station. If one team member runs ahead, a 1-minute penalty will apply, as this changes the dynamic of a Doubles race." (Doubles p. 25)
- So both partners run every run. (Doubles p. 25)
- The books do not set a maximum gap in metres between partners. (Doubles p. 25)
- "If a Doubles Team accrues more than 3 penalties for not staying together, they will be deemed Out Of Competition and will not receive A RANKING." (Doubles p. 25)

### Entering and leaving stations

- "After the run, both team members must enter the workout station together. The workout cannot begin until both are present. The station can only be exited once all reps or distance are completed, and both team members must leave together." (Doubles p. 25)

### Sharing the work

- The book gives a "You Go I Go [YGIG]" example for the 1,000 m row: partner 1 rows while partner 2 waits. "After a self selected distance [E.G. 250 METERS]" they switch. Partner 2 rows while partner 1 rests. They repeat until 1,000 m is done. (Doubles p. 24)
- The book does not set a minimum or maximum share for either partner. It does not require equal work. (Doubles p. 24–38)
- The book never says in one sentence that only one partner may work at a time. **Note:** the YGIG example and the repeated "working DTM / resting DTM" wording show one partner works while the other rests. (Doubles p. 24, p. 27–37)

### Resting partner rules

- "During all workout stations, the resting athlete must remain on their feet. Any down-to-the-ground position - including kneeling, sitting, lying down, or having a knee or any other body part touch the floor - is not permitted. The resting athlete must stay within the designated workout zone without obstructing other racers." (Doubles p. 24)
- Per-station rules for the resting partner and for hand-offs are listed in section 4 under "Doubles extras". In short:
  - SkiErg: stay in the marked area; no passing the handles. (Doubles p. 27)
  - Sled Push: walk "immediately behind" the working partner. (Doubles p. 28)
  - Sled Pull: stay behind; no help with the rope; verbal and physical guidance allowed if they stay off and out of the Racer's Box. (Doubles p. 29)
  - Burpee Broad Jump: walk behind; new worker places hands where the last worker's feet landed. (Doubles p. 31)
  - Rowing: stay in the marked area behind; no passing the handle or setting the damper or straps for each other. (Doubles p. 32)
  - Farmers Carry and Lunges: walk behind; kettlebells and sandbag must never be passed forward. (Doubles p. 33, p. 35)
  - Wall Balls: stay in the marked area under the rig; hand the ball over or let it drop; no flying transition. (Doubles p. 36)

### Finish and age group

- The official finish time is taken when both partners cross the line. The later chip read counts. (Doubles p. 10)
- A team's age group is "determined by their average age on the day of their race." (Doubles p. 10)

### Pro Doubles nationality rule (for Elite points)

- Both partners in a Pro Doubles team must hold the same nationality, shown by a valid, unexpired passport from the same country. Residency or visa status does not count. (Doubles p. 13, p. 20)
- Teams must keep their passports available on race day. (Doubles p. 13)
- Dual citizens compete under one nationality only. Changing it forfeits points earned under the old one. (Doubles p. 13, p. 20)

## 7. Relay rules

### Team

- "HYROX TEAM RELAY is a 4-person-team race." (Relay p. 10)
- Make-up: 4 women, 4 men, or 2 women and 2 men. (Relay p. 10)
- "A HYROX Relay Team must consist of four racers. Starting the race with fewer than four racers present to participate is not permitted" (Relay p. 10)
- Each member wears a chip: "Relay = 4 Timing Chips". (Relay p. 30)

### Who does what

- "Each RM has to run 2 x 1 km and perform 2 workout stations." RM means Relay Member. (Relay p. 8, p. 10)
- "Which run/workstations each RM completes is entirely up to the team." (Relay p. 10)
- A member may do 2 runs and 2 stations back to back. Or they may do one, return to the Transition Zone, and do another later. (Relay p. 10)
- "For Teams, only one RM is active at a time whilst the other three are waiting for their turn." (Relay p. 12)
- Each leg is a run followed by its station. **Note:** the book's protocol describes the next member starting "their run" after the hand-off, so each member does run-then-station. (Relay p. 13)

### Transition Zone (TZ)

- "The Transition Zone [TZ] is where the RM exchange takes place." (Relay p. 13)
- "The RM who is up next is only allowed to enter shortly before the exchange. No loitering is allowed in this zone." (Relay p. 13)
- The team must make sure members reach the TZ on time. (Relay p. 13)
- After finishing a station, the member must run into the TZ to meet the next member. (Relay p. 13)
- Exchange protocol: "When RM 1 finishes their run and workout station, they enter the TZ. They then high-five RM 2 who is waiting for them. Once the transition occurs, RM2 starts their run and RM 1 promptly exits the TZ to avoid congestion." (Relay p. 13)
- Back-to-back legs: the member must "run through the TZ after each workout to ensure the timing chip is 'seen'. Not doing so will result in an automatic penalty." The penalty size is not given. (Relay p. 13)
- The book does not say where the TZ is on the course. (Relay p. 13)

### Finish

- After the final member "has left the Transition Zone to begin Run 8", the other three go to the wall-ball station by the spectator paths. (Relay p. 25)
- They enter at a "Relay Entry Point" only after the final member has started wall balls. They "stand under the rig to support them". (Relay p. 25)
- "After completing the 100 Wall Balls, all four teammates must run together to the Finisher Stage to cross the finish line as a team." (Relay p. 25)
- The book does not say which chip read sets the team's official finish time. (Relay p. 25, p. 30)

### Other

- Start waves run "every 5 or 10 minutes, depending on the division". (Relay p. 10)
- The Relay book has no World Championship or Elite section. (Relay pp. 2–3)

## 8. Adaptive rules

**Important for any coaching tool:** a coach, an agent, or this skill must not assign an Adaptive division or decide eligibility. The rulebook says racers "must be officially classified into one of the above divisions to ensure eligibility" (Adaptive p. 15). Only the official process can place an athlete. A coach may record the division the athlete was officially given.

### Who decides eligibility, and how

- "Racers must be officially classified into one of the above divisions to ensure eligibility. These divisions are reserved for individuals with permanent impairments." (Adaptive p. 15)
- "Racers with temporary injuries (e.g., due to knee or hip surgery) do not qualify for Adaptive HYROX categories." (Adaptive p. 15)
- Registration step: "Select your adaptive division". (Adaptive p. 6)
- If an impairment does not fit a listed division, "we encourage you to contact us directly". (Adaptive p. 15)
- The book does **not** say who carries out the official classification. It does not describe the process. It does not list what medical evidence is needed. (Adaptive pp. 6–15, 52–56)
- Racers "must declare all specialised equipment during registration." (Adaptive p. 19)
- "Approved adaptations will be prepared in advance, which may include a designated machine, lane, spot if necessary." (Adaptive p. 23)
- By registering, athletes confirm their equipment meets the rulebook's standards. Compliance currently "rests with the athlete". (Adaptive p. 56)
- Classification Codes come in "By the mid-season". A mid-season review will assess them. (Adaptive p. 52, p. 54)

### Permitted modifications

These are listed by station in section 4 under "Adaptive extras". (Adaptive p. 24–41)

### Equipment and assistance

- "Adaptive Equipment is defined as, any item used to compensate for an eligible permanent impairment. This includes prosthetics, wheelchairs, visual/hearing aids, and specialised grip aids." (Adaptive p. 19)
- Equipment "must not provide a mechanical advantage that exceeds the natural movement of a racer." (Adaptive p. 19)
- The racer is liable for their equipment's strength. (Adaptive p. 19)
- Daily-use chairs are allowed; sports wheelchairs are encouraged. (Adaptive p. 20)
- Vision Impaired racers must use a tether. It must be non-elastic and about arm's length, "approx. 50cm". (Adaptive p. 20)
- If equipment fails, the athlete has "a reasonable window to fix it". If it cannot be fixed safely or quickly, they must withdraw. (Adaptive p. 20)
- "No time allowances or deductions will be given for the setup, attachment, removal or handling of adaptive equipment." (Adaptive p. 20)
- Guide runners: "All racers are permitted to complete the race with a guide runner, handler or support person to assist with orientation and navigation." (Adaptive p. 21)
- The racer appoints the guide. HYROX does not provide one, "except in specific cases where local legal requirements apply". (Adaptive p. 21)
- Guides "will not appear in the rankings and are not permitted to assist with the actual execution of workout stations". (Adaptive p. 21)

- The "Adaptive HYROX season" is a qualification window HYROX sets in advance. (Adaptive p. 17)

## 9. Age groups and ranking

### Ranking rules (all formats)

- Overall ranking is by fastest time in the division at each event, fastest to slowest. There is also an age-group ranking. (Singles p. 10; Doubles p. 10; Relay p. 9; Adaptive p. 16)
- If someone races twice in the same division, only their fastest time counts. They can podium only once in that division at that race. (Singles p. 10; Doubles p. 10; Relay p. 9; Adaptive p. 16)
- "Official rankings will be based on racers' biological sex at birth." People racing only for fun may register under their gender identity and are then left out of official rankings. (Singles p. 5; Doubles p. 5; Relay p. 5; Adaptive p. 5)
- Minimum age: "at least 16 years old on the day of race." (Singles p. 5; Doubles p. 5; Relay p. 5; Adaptive p. 5)

### Singles age groups

- UNDER 24 [16-24], 25-29, 30-34, 35-39, 40-44, 45-49, 50-54, 55-59, 60-64*, 65-69*, 70-74*, 75-79*, 80-84*, 85-89*. (Singles p. 9)
- Age group = age on the day of the race. (Singles p. 9)
- Racers may enter Pro divisions "up to and including the 80+ Age Groups at regular HYROX races". Age-group rankings, podiums, and flags apply as normal. (Singles p. 9)
- The * groups: for World Championship qualification, athletes 60 and over qualify through the Open Division only. At the World Championships, the 60+ groups use Open weights, reps, and distances. (Singles p. 9, p. 11, p. 13–14)
- Pro qualifiers aged U24–59 must race Pro distances, reps, and weights at the World Championships. (Singles p. 11)
- At the World Championships the age group is "set by the racer's age at the qualifying event". (Singles p. 13). They race in the age group they qualified in, "regardless of their birthday". (Singles p. 11)
- Elite 15 racers are left out of the age-group rankings at the World Championships. (Singles p. 14)

### Doubles age groups

- Same list as Singles, titled "HYROX AGE GROUPS [EXCLUDING PRO DIVISIONS*]". (Doubles p. 9)
- Team age group = "average age on the day of their race". (Doubles p. 10)
- Teams may race Pro "up to and including the 80+ Age Groups". Age-group rankings, podiums, and flags apply. (Doubles p. 9)
- Teams aged 60+ qualify for the World Championships through Open only and race with Open weights there. "This only applies to the divisions of Doubles Men Pro and Doubles Women Pro". (Doubles p. 10, p. 14)

### Relay age groups

- "UNDER 40" and "40+". (Relay p. 9)
- Based on the average age of the four members at the event. Example: ages 24, 38, 48, and 40 average 37.5, so the team is UNDER 40. (Relay p. 9)

### Adaptive age groups

- The Adaptive book says there is "a ranking within the age groups" but never lists the age groups. (Adaptive p. 4, p. 16)

### World Championship age-group slots (Singles and Doubles)

- Each division (race class) at each qualifying event offers one World Championship spot. (Singles p. 11–12; Doubles p. 12)
- Offers go out by email. The athlete or team has 48 hours to accept. (Singles p. 11–12; Doubles p. 11–12)
- At most two non-licence holders (or pairs) are offered a slot per race class per event. (Singles p. 12; Doubles p. 11–12)
- Three declines in the same division exclude the athlete for the rest of the season. (Singles p. 12; Doubles p. 12)
- An accepted slot may be forfeited once per division per season. (Singles p. 12; Doubles p. 12–13)
- Racers may enter several divisions at the World Championships if start times do not clash. Start-time changes are not considered. (Singles p. 13; Doubles p. 13)

### Elite 15 qualification (short version)

- Points-based, over a rolling 365-day window. Singles: best 5 results count. Doubles: best 3. (Singles p. 15–16; Doubles p. 15–16)
- A valid HYROX Athlete Licence is required to earn points. It must be applied for before the race. It cannot be applied retrospectively. (Singles p. 16; Doubles p. 16)
- Event 1 invites the top 15 at the cut-off. Events 2–5 invite the top 14, plus the previous Challenger winner from Race 3 onward. (Singles p. 15–16; Doubles p. 15–16)
- Each Elite 15 race offers 3 World Championship slots. (Singles p. 15, p. 21; Doubles p. 15, p. 21)
- Points: fixed points for top places, then "1ST PLACE TIME / NTH PLACE TIME X 100". A finisher must be within a set percentage of the winner's time (10% up to 20%, depending on place). Once one finisher is outside the limit, no later finisher scores. (Singles p. 17–19; Doubles p. 17–19)
- Fixed top points: Standard Pro Race 105, 100; Challenger 108, 106, 103, 100; Major Elite 15 Race 110 down to 100 for 10th; Elite 15 World Championship Race 115, 112, 110, 109, 108, 107, 106, 105, 103, 100. (Singles p. 17–19; Doubles p. 17–19)
- Ties are broken by "Strength of Field", then a "sudden death review" of counting results. (Singles p. 19; Doubles p. 19)

## 10. Other facts useful for pacing and strategy

### Start

- Wave starts "at regular scheduled intervals [e.g., every 10 minutes]". (Singles p. 22; Doubles p. 23; Adaptive p. 18). Relay: "every 5 or 10 minutes, depending on the division". (Relay p. 10)
- Racers must show their start time on their arm. (Singles p. 22)
- Be at the start tunnel area 10 minutes before the start time. (Singles p. 42; Doubles p. 44; Relay p. 31; Adaptive p. 47)
- Move forward with the start wave. Do not wait in the tunnel. (Singles p. 24)
- Do not enter the Roxzone or run course before the start time. It may trigger the chip. (Singles p. 22)
- Wristbands are "white, grey or black and/or green" and show start wave and division. (Singles p. 41)
- Bring government photo ID and the registration confirmation. (Singles p. 41)
- A warm-up area with race equipment is available. Spectators are not allowed in it. (Singles p. 42; Doubles p. 44; Relay p. 31; Adaptive p. 47)

### Machines

- SkiErg damper: preset to 6; the racer may change it as often as they like. (Singles p. 26; Doubles p. 27 working partner only; Adaptive p. 24). No note in Relay. (Relay p. 15)
- Rower damper: preset to 6; may be changed as often as desired. (Singles p. 30; Relay p. 19; Adaptive p. 32). Doubles: working partner only. (Doubles p. 32)
- Rower footplates: preset to Position Number 4; may be adjusted "before beginning". (Singles p. 30; Doubles p. 32; Relay p. 19; Adaptive p. 32)
- Racers may stop rowing and rest with the handle in the holder, but must stay seated. (Singles p. 30)
- A judge resets each machine monitor before the start. (Singles p. 26, p. 30)

### Pacing-relevant movement permissions

- SkiErg: jumping or dynamic motion is allowed. (Singles p. 26)
- Burpee Broad Jump: may step or jump out of the burpee; may use a knee; jump length is free. (Singles p. 29)
- Farmers Carry: may put kettlebells down to rest if they do not move forward. (Singles p. 31)
- Lunges: may stop between lunges with feet parallel. (Singles p. 32)
- Wall Balls: may catch the ball or let it drop and restart from standing. (Singles p. 34)
- Overtaking is allowed on Lunges, Burpee Broad Jumps, and Farmers Carry. (Singles p. 25)
- Racers count their own laps. Lap screens are not official. Extra laps get no time back. (Singles p. 22)

### Chalk

- Only event-supplied chalk, only where it is provided. (Singles p. 23)
- "CHALK USE IS PERMITTED JUST FOR SLED PULL AND FARMERS CARRY." Elsewhere: 2 minutes. (Singles p. 45)
- Powdered chalk at wall balls: 2 minutes. (Singles p. 36)

### Water and food

- Water is available "at least once during, before or after each pass through the Roxzone". A sports drink "may also be available". (Singles p. 40)
- "Any racers desiring nutrition must carry it on their person from the start of the race." (Singles p. 40)
- No drinks or food from anyone except aid stations. (Singles p. 40)
- Aid-station fluids are for drinking only. No pouring on the body. (Singles p. 23)
- Throw cups and gel wrappers in the bins. (Singles p. 40)

### Clothing and kit

- Closed-toe shoes at all times, except at wall balls, where shoes may come off. (Singles p. 38)
- Allowed: knee sleeves, "Gloves [not grips]", weightlifting belt, wristbands, hydration packs, asthma inhalers or similar prescribed respiratory devices. (Singles p. 38)
- "Any item not explicitly listed as permitted is, by default, prohibited." Written permission from the Race Director is needed for anything else. (Singles p. 38–39)
- Any permitted item used must be used from start to finish. It cannot be handed to or from anyone. (Singles p. 38)
- Forbidden: headphones, cell phones, VR headsets, body cameras, helmets, breathing apparatus, compressed air cylinders, ear plugs, and smart or camera glasses. (Singles p. 39)
- A medical device that needs a phone nearby needs the Race Director's approval before the race. (Singles p. 39)
- "UNIFORMS AND WEIGHT VESTS ARE STILL PERMITTED, PROVIDED THEY DO NOT INTERFERE WITH THE SAFETY OR PERFORMANCE OF OTHER RACERS." (Singles p. 39)
- Weight colours: WHITE = Women; GREY = Women Pro and Men; BLACK = Men Pro. (Singles p. 32–35)

### Anti-doping

- Racers must accept testing in and out of competition. "TUE's [Therapeutic Use Exemptions] can be submitted 28 days prior to racing." (Singles p. 37; Doubles p. 39; Relay p. 26; Adaptive p. 42)

### Pregnancy and injury

- Pregnant racers may take part. Any change to movements, weights, or reps must be arranged with HYROX in advance and makes the result "Out of Competition". (Singles p. 5–6)
- Injured racers need medical clearance. Any injury-related change makes the result "Out of Competition". (Singles p. 6)

## Ambiguities and contradictions

1. **No real version marker.** No book prints a date or version number. Only "SEASON 26/27" identifies them. (Singles p. 1; Doubles p. 1; Relay p. 1; Adaptive p. 1, p. 52)
2. **Roxzone and splits are undefined.** No book says where the Roxzone starts or ends. No book lists official split types. (Singles p. 22–23, p. 43)
3. **Incomplete station: DQ or DNF?** Section 8 and the penalty table say an incomplete station is DQ. But 12.3 says a racer who "does not finish a station" may "continue with the race [without a final time]". And 12.4 says a DQ racer may not continue. (Singles p. 25, p. 44–45; same in Doubles and Relay)
4. **Doubles machines vs YGIG.** Doubles SkiErg and Rowing rules say the athlete may not leave the machine until the full distance is done and a judge confirms. Yet the YGIG example has partners switching mid-row. (Doubles p. 24, p. 27, p. 32)
5. **Doubles age groups title.** The heading says "[EXCLUDING PRO DIVISIONS*]". The text below says Pro teams get age-group rankings, podiums, and flags. (Doubles p. 9)
6. **Doubles slot count.** 5.1 says the number of slots "will be announced on the event page". 5.3 says each division offers "one World Championship qualification spot". (Doubles p. 11–12)
7. **"80+ Age Groups"** is used for Pro entry, but the listed groups are 80-84 and 85-89. (Singles p. 9; Doubles p. 9)
8. **Doubles "not staying together" penalty** appears in the Doubles text (1 minute, more than 3 means Out Of Competition) but not in the Doubles penalty table. It appears in the Adaptive table as code 17, though Adaptive has no Doubles format yet. (Doubles p. 25, p. 47; Adaptive p. 50, p. 55)
9. **Relay Mixed loads** are not stated. The table has only Women and Men columns. (Relay p. 8)
10. **Relay order.** "RM4" and "Run 8" suggest a fixed fourth member for the last leg. But run order is "entirely up to the team". (Relay p. 10, p. 25)
11. **Relay gaps.** The size of the TZ "automatic penalty", the TZ location, and how the team finish time is read are not stated. (Relay p. 13, p. 25)
12. **Relay SkiErg damper** has no note. Other books preset it to 6. (Relay p. 15; Singles p. 26)
13. **Chalk wording.** 7.1 allows chalk "at the designated stations where it is provided". The penalty table says chalk is allowed "JUST FOR SLED PULL AND FARMERS CARRY". (Singles p. 23, p. 45)
14. **Adaptive digital wall-ball targets** count reps automatically and racers go when the screen shows the reps. The other books say screens "do not determine the validity of repetitions". (Adaptive p. 40; Singles p. 35)
15. **Adaptive SkiErg and rower shortfall.** The station text gives 15 seconds per metre not done. The table gives "MIN PENALTY 10 SECS TO DQ". (Adaptive p. 24, p. 32, p. 50)
16. **Adaptive undeclared equipment.** One page says time penalty or DQ. The appendix says the Head Judge "may adjust their rank". (Adaptive p. 19, p. 56)
17. **Adaptive missing pieces.** Section 2.4 is in the contents but not in the body. Age groups are mentioned but not listed. The classifier and the classification process are not named. "1PT" and "2PT" are not defined. (Adaptive p. 2, p. 5–6, p. 10–11, p. 15–16)
18. **Adaptive lap distances.** "1 LAP" and "2 LAPS" have no metre value and depend on the venue. (Adaptive p. 21)
19. **Burpee 30 cm wording.** The 30 cm measuring note sits under the overstepping rule, but 30 cm is the hand-placement limit in the next rule. (Singles p. 29)
20. **Doubles wording.** "Both Doubles Teams" is used where "both athletes" is meant. (Doubles p. 13, p. 20)
21. **Elite tier names differ.** Singles lists "Elite - Racing Series". Doubles lists "Elite - Major Race". The points table in both says "MAJOR ELITE 15 RACE". (Singles p. 17–18; Doubles p. 17–18)
22. **Fast Lane unit.** "4/km pace" has no unit printed. (Singles p. 23)
